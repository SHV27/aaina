## ✅ PPRS — Perceived Partner Responsiveness (Reis, Crasta, Rogge, Maniaci & Carmichael 2018)
Source: author-distributed PDF, Rochester Relationship Lab. **"Freely available to researchers with appropriate citation."**
Stem "My partner usually:" · 9-point 1 not at all true → 9 completely true (5/7-pt variants used) · sum · α .91–.98 · no reverse items.
General: G1 really listens to me* · G2 is responsive to my needs*
Understanding: U1 is an excellent judge of my character · U2 sees the "real" me* · U3 sees the same virtues and faults in me as I see in myself · U4 "gets the facts right" about me* · U5 is aware of what I am thinking and feeling · U6 understands me* · U7 is on "the same wavelength" with me* · U8 knows me well*
Validation: V1 esteems me, shortcomings and all* · V2 values and respects the whole package that is the "real" me* · V3 usually seems to focus on the "best side" of me · V4 expresses liking and encouragement for me* · V5 seems interested in what I am thinking and feeling* · V6 seems interested in doing things with me · V7 values my abilities and opinions* · V8 respects me
(* = the 12-item version, Reis et al. 2011)

## ✅ GMSEX (Lawrance & Byers 1995)
"In general, how would you describe your sexual relationship with your partner?" — 5 bipolar pairs, 7-point: Good–Bad · Pleasant–Unpleasant · Positive–Negative · Satisfying–Unsatisfying · Valuable–Worthless. Sum 5–35, α > .90.

## ✅ Trust in Close Relationships (Rempel, Holmes & Zanna 1985) — 17 items, −3..+3
DEPENDABILITY: D1 trustworthy/willing to let them do threatening activities · D2 unusually dependable on things important to me · D3 certain would not cheat even if no chance of being caught · D4 can rely on to keep promises · D5 confident telling truth even when excuses sound unlikely
FAITH: F1 comfortable telling anything, even shameful · F2 always ready to offer strength and support · F3 concerned about my welfare in new decisions · F4 certain will share things even with no reason to expect it · F5 reacts positively when I expose weaknesses · F6 responds lovingly before I say anything · F7 secure facing unknown situations together
PREDICTABILITY: P1(R) never certain won't do something I dislike/embarrass · P2(R) very unpredictable, never know how they'll act · P3(R) uncomfortable when they make decisions affecting me · P4 behaves in a very consistent manner · P5(R) sometimes avoid them because unpredictable/fear conflict
(Note: PsyToolkit's subscale headers are scrambled; grouping above corrected.)

## ✅ Partner Phubbing Scale (Roberts & David 2016) — 9 items, 1 never → 5 all the time, item 7 reverse, α .93
1 mealtime pulls out and checks phone · 2 places phone where they can see it · 3 keeps phone in hand · 4 pulls it out mid-conversation when it rings/beeps · 5 glances at phone when talking to me · 6 uses phone during leisure time together · 7(R) does not use phone when we are talking · 8 uses phone when we are out together · 9 checks phone during a lull in conversation
⚠️ his/her phrasing varies across press sources vs CHB Table 2.

## ✅ FOBS — Fear of Being Single (Spielmann et al. 2013) — 6 items, 1 not at all true → 5 very true, mean, α .83–.87
1 close to being too late to find the love of my life · 2 anxious thinking about being single forever · 3 need to find a partner before too old to have/raise children · 4 if I end up alone, something is wrong with me · 5 as I get older it gets harder to find someone · 6 scares me there might not be anyone out there
(R-FOBS variant exists for partnered people — "If I were single, it would be…" — use for our partnered users.)

## ✅ GHOST (Jahrami et al. 2023, Heliyon, CC-BY — free reuse with attribution) — 8 items, 1 never → 5 always, sum 8–40, α .88
1 stood up / plans cancelled without being told beforehand · 2 reply messages delayed · 3 replies confusing and vague · 4 blocked or deleted from social/messaging apps · 5 "I'm busy" always used · 6 interest inconsistent — sometimes engaged, sometimes uninterested · 7 doesn't share personal information · 8 not interested in meeting

## ⚠️ Tokunaga IES (2011) — 7-point (NOT 5), 15-item pool. Verified verbatim: monitor partner's social networking site · check up through profile updates · aware of relationships between partner and their SNS friends · investigate confusing wall messages through others' profiles. Full set paywalled.

## ⚠️ Muise Facebook Jealousy (2009) — 27 items, 7-point 1 very unlikely → 7 very likely, α .96, stem "How likely are you to…". Verified verbatim: become jealous after partner added unknown member of opposite sex · monitor partner's activities on Facebook · feel threatened if partner added a previous romantic/sexual partner · look at partner's page if suspicious. Full list never published openly. Also verified single-item surveillance measure (Marshall 2013): "How often do you look at your partner's page?" 1 never → 5 very often.

## ⚠️ Knobloch & Solomon relational uncertainty — stem "How certain are you about…", 6-point 1 completely uncertain → 6 completely certain, reverse-scored so higher = more uncertainty. Verified exemplars: how committed you are to this relationship (self) · how committed your partner is (partner) · the future of this relationship (relationship). Content domains verified: desire, evaluation, goals (self/partner); behavioral norms, mutuality of feelings, current definition, future (relationship). Full 54-item appendix paywalled.

# v2 Lane 4 — Deep-Report Craft, Support Patterns, Voice (verified 2026-08-24)

## Structural techniques (named)
- **A. Summary-then-descent** (Hogan, Pointerpro): one-screen orientation of the whole finding before detail → verdict card first, then chapters.
- **B. Staged reveal as narrative arc** (Spotify Wrapped): interpret/contextualize/connect-to-identity, never just report. Resolution with A: **staged reveal for the emotional first pass; scannable document for the re-read** (23andMe does this split).
- **C. Full ranking with a fold** (CliftonStrengths 34): show the complete ranked landscape, design attention around top/bottom extremes.
- **D. Per-dimension chapter template** (16p premium): score viz → what it means FOR YOU (echo actual answers) → practical tips INSIDE the chapter, never in a back-matter appendix. Granularity itself reads as rigor (30 facets vs 5 dimensions).
- **E. Bright side / dark side / inside** (Hogan): "how you show up / how you show up when it's bad / what you actually need" — strongest structural steal for a relationship report.
- **F. Personalization signals**: name in headers/charts, quote user's own data back ("you said X in Q12"). Listing scores is explicitly insufficient.
- **G. Coaching-plan close** (Hogan): last chapter is a worksheet, not a conclusion.

## Long-read UX rules
1. TOC at top with in-page anchors (NN/g reversed old advice; buried TOCs fail).
2. Max TWO disclosure levels (three+ loses users). Accordions per dimension = one level, no nesting.
3. Progressive disclosure: 20–40% faster completion, better comprehension. Collapse "the science behind this."
4. One idea per screen-height; bolded one-sentence takeaway per chapter for skimmers (the majority).
5. Numerals+viz for comparison/location; prose for meaning/instruction. Never a naked score without adjacent interpretation.
6. **Pull-quotes = the user's own words** — proves it was written for them.
7. Chapter x of y + thin progress bar beat time estimates; pacing IS the experience.

## Support-system patterns (no accounts, no notification spam)
- **Retake interval as product** (Gottman Checkup bakes in 3–4 month retake; the DELTA is the value) → dated "retake on [date]" + diff view; first take stored locally/exportable code.
- **Structured action plan as exit artifact**, printable, SMALL (3 actions not 12).
- **Shareable summary as re-entry vector** (Wrapped's retention core): WhatsApp-forwardable verdict card, sensitive detail stripped — also the mechanism by which a partner takes the test.
- **Trigger–action–investment without notifications**: user-owned triggers (printed plan, .ics calendar check-in file, retake date); investment = locally-persisted annotate/check-off making the report a living document.

## Voice rules — "warm bro with a Harvard PhD"
1. Protagonist-and-obstacle framing (Gottlieb): what do they want, what blocks it — cut everything else.
2. Zoom out, then back in (Savage): specific → general truth → back to THEIR detail.
3. Second person, warm not accusing; pair every "you do X" with the motive underneath.
4. "You might be the problem" via reframe, never verdict (Perel): pattern → what it protects → what it costs. Never "you are avoidant."
5. Short declaratives carry hard lines; rhythm = long explanatory sentence → short verdict sentence.
6. Cite science as a friend would: named researcher + finding in one breath, zero jargon (no "dysregulation", "holding space").
7. Humor at situations/behaviours, never at pain; no jokes in the hardest section.
8. Preserve agency: "Here's what I see. You know things I don't."
9. Uncomfortable truths framed as acts of care (School of Life).
10. One idea per paragraph; ask of every sentence "is this extraneous?"

## Banned style (AI tells)
Phrases: "it's important to note", "delve/dive into", "navigate the complexities/landscape", "embark on a journey", "in today's fast-paced world", "tapestry", "fosters", "at the end of the day", "game-changer/transformative/groundbreaking", "holistic", "empower/unlock/unleash", "furthermore/moreover/additionally" as paragraph openers, "in conclusion".
Structures: hedging stacks; tricolon abuse; "it's not X, it's Y" more than once per report; generic empathy filler ("relationships are hard", "every couple is unique"); praise sandwiches; bold-everything lists; uplift sentence ending every section; any claim without a user-data citation.
